## 1. PANEL WORK — the base table

**WHOLE JOB — labour, paint and materials.**

| grade | meaning | hours | **all-in £** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MINOR** | surface scuff, abrasion, paint transfer | 2 | **£200** |
| **MODERATE** | genuine dent or crease, repairable | 6 | **£700** |
| **SEVERE** | torn, crushed, deformed — replace and repaint | 5 | **£600** |

⚠️ **On SEVERE the PART cost is separate.** ✅ **ONE TABLE FOR EVERY BOLT-ON PANEL
WHATEVER THE SIZE** — *"Yes… same"* to bonnet, sill and bumper. ⛔ **No size multiplier.**

🔴 **11 Sep — THIS LINE WENT UNHONOURED FOR ELEVEN DAYS.** "PART cost separate" is
written against SEVERE and nowhere else: a MINOR or MODERATE bolt-on panel is
**repaired**, £700 all-in, and **buys no new panel**. The pricing charged a replacement
part on every grade. ⚖️ **Vincent, 11 Sep, from the SD72HXH photographs: *"All the
panels are repairable, shouldn't be replaced."*** Built in batch 116 — the grade owns
repair-vs-replace and the model's action word stops deciding money. **£640 on one lot;
−£670 across the stored corpus, which is fourteen heavy front hits and badly
under-represents the light-damage lots this bites.**

### 🔴 WELDED AND BONDED PANELS — revised by Vincent, 31 Aug

| welded / bonded panel | **REPLACE** | **REPAIR** |
|---|---|---|
| **Quarter panel** | **£800** | **£800** — *"repair or replace, not much difference"* |
| **Sill** | **£800** | **£700** |
| **Roof skin** | **£1,500** | **£1,000** |

📌 **The roof is £1,500 to replace because *"roof skin usually glue on now"*** — modern
skins are BONDED, not welded. A specialist job. Sill replace rose to £800.
📌 **These INCLUDE repairing the structure behind the panel** — *"usually if the damage
is bad enough to warrant replacement, the structure it attaches to will need repair."*
⚠️ **COWORK READING:** MINOR on a welded panel stays £200, from his explicit "a scuffed
sill is the same £200 as a scuffed wing."
### ⚖️ RULED 14 SEPTEMBER 2026 — THE WELDED PART COST

> **Vincent: *"Very rarely would someone use a s/h weld on part so no s/h column needed
> and new weld in needs a cost."*** · *"Repair = labour only, no panel — yes."*

1. 🔴 **WELDED PANELS PRICE AT NEW. There is no second-hand figure for them.** A
   colour-matched used quarter, sill or roof skin is not something the trade welds in.
   ⛔ **Do not read the `used` column for QUARTER PANEL, SILL or ROOF SKIN — read `oem`.**
2. **A welded REPLACE carries a new panel part cost**, on top of the labour figure above.
3. **A welded REPAIR is LABOUR ONLY and buys NO panel.** You beat and fill the panel that
   is already on the car; nothing is bought.

💷 **Money effect, both directions — must be MEASURED, not assumed:**

| | now | ruled | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rear quarter, Executive band | £175 (`used`) | **£320** (`oem`) | **+£145** |
| Sill, Executive band | £75 (`used`) | **£140** (`oem`) | **+£65** |
| Three corpus quarters actioned "repair" | £615 of panel | **£0** | **−£615** |

⚠️ **Replaces go UP, repairs lose their part. The corpus net is unknown until measured.**

📌 **The principle generalises: a panel being repaired buys nothing.** That is the same
rule batch 116 applied to bolt-ons, now extended to welded. It also condemns §4's
sourced-finished column adding £170 to repaired bolt-ons — see open item 4.

### 🎯 THE RULE THIS ESTABLISHES — the direction INVERTS by panel type

| panel type | which costs more | why |
|---|---|---|
| **BOLT-ON** | **REPAIR > replace** — MODERATE £700 > SEVERE £600 | unbolting and hanging a new one is quicker than beating a dent out |
| **WELDED / BONDED** | **REPLACE ≥ repair** — sill £800 > £700 · roof £1,500 > £1,000 · quarter equal | cutting out and welding or bonding in a new panel is a major job |

⛔ **DO NOT NORMALISE THESE TO ONE RULE.** ⚠️ **The severity grade alone no longer
decides the figure — the PANEL CLASS selects which direction applies. The welded set is
exactly QUARTER PANEL, SILL, ROOF SKIN; everything else is bolt-on.**

📌 **Recorded because it looks like an inconsistency and is not:** Cowork flagged the
earlier figures as "not following one rule", which is what prompted Vincent to revise
them. **The inversion is deliberate.**

## 4. SOURCED-FINISHED — bolt-on only

**Bumpers, doors, wings, bonnets, boot lids** come finished and colour-matched:
**≈ £170 a panel.** **Quarters, sills, roofs cannot.**

⚠️ **£170 IS A MID FIGURE.** *"On some cars you fit a door in 30 minutes; others with
elec windows, central locking, elec door mirror could take 4 hours."* 📌 **Which is why
the model outputs a range.**

⚠️ **11 Sep: this column is computed and displayed NOWHERE**, and it adds £170 for every
bolt-on including panels being *repaired*, which buy no panel at all. Display-only, so no
money is wrong.

### ⚖️ RULED 14 SEPTEMBER 2026 — FIX IT, THEN SHOW IT

> **Vincent: *"Show it fixed."***

**This column is the trade route: buy a colour-matched used bolt-on and clip it on,
instead of buying new and painting.** It is the cheaper way the job actually gets done,
and it is the number this product's buyer most wants to see.

1. 🔴 **FIX FIRST: strip repaired panels out of it.** A repaired panel gets no new panel
   fitted, so there is nothing to fit and no £170. **Same principle as the welded ruling —
   a panel being repaired buys nothing.**
2. **THEN DISPLAY IT** as a second figure beside the main one: *new and painted £X — or
   about £Y sourcing finished panels.*
3. ⛔ **It stays DISPLAY ONLY and never drives the bid** (§11). **No money moves.**

📌 **Bolt-on only, and that agrees with the welded ruling in §1** — a quarter, sill or
roof cannot be sourced finished and cannot be second-hand. **The two rulings describe the
same trade fact from opposite ends.**

📌 **Build with the §6 range display — same surfaces, same work.** Both are figures the
engine already computes, stores, and shows to nobody.

## 5. 🎯 THE STRUCTURAL ALLOWANCE

**Engine-out · jigging for wheel alignment · chassis repair.** ⛔ **A per-panel table can
never produce this.**

### ⚖️ RULED 11 SEPTEMBER 2026 — SUPERSEDES THE FLAT BAND BELOW

> **Vincent: *"Jigging costs are nearly impossible to predict. I said from £500 up to 2
> or 3k depending on the vehicle and the extent of the damage."***

**THE RULE:**
1. **£500 is a FLOOR and it goes in the repair total.** Defensible, never inflating the
   figure with a guess, never leaving a hole.
2. **The buyer-facing wording carries the real range** — from £500, and on a heavier hit
   or a larger vehicle **£2,000–£3,000**, quotable only after inspection. ⛔ **"From £500"
   alone is not enough: a buyer reading it budgets five hundred pounds when the true
   figure may be six times that.**
3. **The range does NOT vary by vehicle in the code.** The no-classifier ruling below
   still stands — nothing in the data reliably separates an SUV. **"Depending on the
   vehicle" belongs in the sentence the buyer reads, not in a band the engine picks.**

⛔ **WITHDRAWN: the flat £2,300–£2,500 default.** Withdrawn in code by batch 95, replaced
on 4 September by the £500 floor (`ZERO_RULE_STRUCT_FLOOR`), and neither copy of this
document was amended for eleven days — it sat marked FINAL and closed, telling every
reader to charge a figure production had not charged since August.

**Display a range · margin, profit window and every bid ceiling compute off the TOP · an
